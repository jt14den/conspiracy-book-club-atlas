- **Address investigation:** a historical address or broader area is supported, but the present parcel or precise site is unresolved.
- **Hold:** the story merits further research, but the location is not established.

## DC01 — Superet Light Church: Mother Trust's atom-aura theology

**Why it belongs:** a surviving Westlake institution where religious history, neon, auras, and the language of atomic science meet. This adds a different story to the existing Wilshire/Westlake circuit.

**Supported finding:** the church's own site lists 2516 W. 3rd Street and presents its light-centered Christian teaching. The Los Angeles Archivists Collective documents Josephine de Croix Trust's purchase of a chapel in 1926, later additions, the purple-heart neon, and her system of colored auras and spiritual atoms. It identifies the site as Cultural-Historic Monument 555. These are evidence of the institution and its teachings, not evidence that aura claims are scientifically true.

**Evidence:** history **Strong** for the core institutional association, using the organization and an archival essay. Geography **Strong** at the publicly listed address; exact parcel/entrance coordinates remain unreviewed. Independent city monument documentation would strengthen the historical construction chronology.

**Map description should emphasize:** Mother Trust, the surviving church, its distinctive theology and visual architecture. Avoid treating the founder's claimed aristocratic status as established biography.

**Access:** an active religious institution. Check its current visitor guidance before arranging a club visit. The HMS Bounty (existing B02) is an optional stop on a larger Westlake/Wilshire outing; this is a geographic pairing.

**Sources read:** [Superet church](https://www.superet.org/); [Mothers of Magic, section on Mother Trust](https://www.laacollective.org/work/mothers-of-magic).

## DC02 — Psychic Temple: the Holy Kiss Society in Long Beach

**Why it belongs:** an identifiable building with an unusually specific early twentieth-century religious history, followed by a hotel and a restoration story.

**Supported finding:** Long Beach historian Stanley Poe's account identifies the 1905 building with William C. Price's New or Practical Psychology group, also called the Holy Kiss Society, and describes the financial dispute with followers and subsequent American Hotel use. The building's own history repeats that account. Downtown Long Beach Alliance supplies the current venue address, 228 E. Broadway. Some accounts use 224 or the 224–230 building range; reconcile the footprint rather than create separate sites.

**Evidence:** core history **Strong**, supported by a named local historian and preservation history. Geography **Strong** for the building. The owner's timeline and Poe article may share a source chain; do not count them as two independent investigations. The city landmark file and contemporary court/newspaper records are the next checks for detailed legal claims.

**Map description should emphasize:** the documented society/building association and later reuse. Terms such as hypnotic control appear in accounts of the dispute; they should not become an unqualified claim that hypnosis caused members' behavior.

**Access:** treat the restored property as a functioning workplace/event building, not a continuously open museum. Existing Bamboo Club (B21) is a Long Beach detour, not an adjacent bar.

**Sources read:** [Long Beach Heritage, Fall 2015, printed page 3](https://www.lbheritage.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/lbh-newsletter-fall-2015.pdf); [building history](https://psychictemplelongbeach.com/history/); [Downtown Long Beach Alliance address](https://downtownlongbeach.org/venue/the-psychic-temple/).

## DC03 — Continental Building: Mankind United and the Hidden Rulers

**Why it belongs:** this is the clearest new connection between a conspiracy-belief movement and a recognizable downtown building.

**Supported finding:** Bob Wolfe's legal-history tour identifies 408 S. Spring Street and reports that Arthur Bell acquired control of the Continental Building in 1944 through a purchase in his wife's maiden name. It connects him with Mankind United and its belief in secret rulers controlling governments and wealth. It also records the building's sale in 1946. The article's citations point to litigation over Bell and the Church of the Golden Rule.

**Evidence:** historical association **Moderate**: a named specialist's researched account, with traceable legal and newspaper citations, but no deed or ownership instrument inspected in this pass. Building identity/address **Strong**. A recorded deed or receiver's asset inventory would upgrade the ownership evidence.

**Map description should emphasize:** an ownership/asset connection in 1944–46. Do not relabel the building a proven ritual center or original movement headquarters. If discussing Bell's wartime sedition case, include that his conviction was reversed in 1947.

**Follow-up:** the published receivership appeal confirms litigation over Bell and the church; the short federal appellate opinion reverses the wartime judgments and orders the indictment dismissed. Neither opinion by itself establishes the Continental ownership transaction. Next obtain the deed or receiver’s asset inventory. The trial files and 1945 newspaper story were not inspected.

**Bar pairing:** La Cita (B14) fits a downtown circuit; no historical connection is asserted.

**Sources read:** [California Supreme Court Historical Society Review, Fall/Winter 2019, printed pages 10–11 and endnotes](https://www.cschs.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/2019-CSCHS-Review-Law-Walk-Part-3.pdf); [published receivership appeal](https://openjurist.org/79/calapp2d/858/people-ex-rel-kenny-v-christ-s-church-of-the-golden-rule-1230326); [Bell v. United States, 159 F.2d 247](https://openjurist.org/159/f2d/247/bell-v-united-states).

## DC04 — Pisgah Home, with a separate Pisgah Grande investigation

Yoakum's religious social-service movement and assistance to poor and unemployed people. The National Register nomination documents the 1914 expansion to Pisgah Grande and describes the Simi site as ruins when the nomination was prepared.

**Evidence:** Home history and district geography **Strong**, using government heritage records. Pisgah Grande's historical association **Strong**, but the precise ruins, parcel, current condition, and lawful approach remain **Limited** in this pass. The nomination is historical evidence, not a 2026 trail-access report.

**Map description should emphasize:** faith healing, social philanthropy, and cooperative settlement. A history of religious experimentation does not itself establish coercion or fraud.

**Next records:** map the Home district separately. For Grande, trace the original landholding and site plan before adding a canyon pin. A Los Angeles County survey abstract surfaced with a reference to the former Pisgah Grande location, but its full survey context was not reviewed; it remains a document lead.

**Bar pairing:** existing La Cuevita (B08) is a Highland Park option. Review walking access across the road/freeway network instead of using straight-line distance.

**Sources read:** [OHP district record](https://ohp.parks.ca.gov/ListedResources/Detail/N2377); [National Register nomination, especially continuation page 17, PDF page 17](https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/e67ee13e-c860-476c-8ac6-d0bfa5f02854). **Further record located, not fully evaluated:** [county survey abstract](https://pw.lacounty.gov/sur/nas/County_Abstracts/7288465.pdf).

## DC05 — San Marino's Birch publishing/office address

**Why it belongs:** a concrete San Gabriel Valley location for the region's anti-communist and conspiracy-belief politics.

**Supported finding:** a February 25, 1975 letter on General Birch Services Corporation stationery lists 2627 Mission Street, San Marino. The letterhead names American Opinion, Western Islands, and other Birch-related publishing and speaking operations. Brown University's John Birch Society finding aid independently confirms a San Marino West Coast office and its closure in 1989. Its historical note describes Robert Welch's allegation that Eisenhower served a communist conspiracy.

**Evidence:** the address association with Birch office/publishing operations in 1975 is **Strong**, based on contemporary letterhead. The precise occupancy dates, suite boundaries, and match to the later West Coast office are **Moderate**. The finding aid corroborates the city and organization, not every detail of this particular building.

**Map description should emphasize:** the dated office/publishing role. Keep the primary document's corporate names rather than flatten all entities into one headquarters. Verify the modern street frontage; present occupants should not inherit the historical organization's identity.

**Next records:** San Marino business directories and the 1989 office-closure reporting. This is a political-history stop, not a religious-cult classification.

**Sources read:** [contemporary letter, Liberty University digital collections](https://liberty.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p17184coll12/id/15905/download); [Brown University finding aid, historical note](https://www.riamco.org/render?eadid=US-RPB-ms2013.003&view=biography).

## DC06 — Daniel Fry's Understanding headquarters in El Monte

**Why it belongs:** a specific local address in the 1950s contactee/UFO milieu, rather than another famous flying-saucer monument outside our present region.

**Supported finding:** the October 1956 issue of *Understanding*, volume 1, number 10, prints 11376 Frankmont Street, El Monte, in its masthead and again explicitly calls it the headquarters address. The issue identifies Daniel W. Fry as editor. The organization's pamphlet traces its beginning to nine members in El Monte in 1955.

**Evidence:** **Strong** for what the organization published about its own address and activities. Current parcel match **Limited**: no period directory, renumbering record, or modern footprint checked. The contemporary issue is available through a later transcription; inspect the original page image before final publication.

**Map description should emphasize:** an organization, publication, and contactee movement. It does not establish an extraterrestrial encounter. A mailing address can identify operations without proving every event occurred there; here the additional headquarters wording is useful evidence, but the exact building use still needs corroboration.

**Next records:** 1956–57 directories, original issue scans, and dated meeting notices. Keep 11376 Frankmont distinct from the earlier 4720 Peck Road correspondence address surfaced in a February issue search. That earlier address was not independently inspected in this pass.

**Sources read:** [October 1956 issue, masthead and Bulletin Board](https://danielfry.com/daniels-writings/understanding-newsletter-1956/vol-1-no-10/); [organizational pamphlet](https://danielfry.com/daniels-writings/understanding/pamphlet/).

## DC08 — Hsi Lai Temple: a bounded campaign-finance history

**Why it belongs:** this adds documented political intrigue in the eastern county. It belongs under political fundraising and investigation, not under a claim that Buddhism or Fo Guang Shan is a cult.

**Supported finding:** the temple's current contact page gives 3456 Glenmark Drive, Hacienda Heights. The Justice Department's Supreme Court brief in *Hsia v. United States*, No. 01-1695, records Maria Hsia's five false-statement convictions and their affirmance, and identifies IBPS as the temple's operator. The underlying episode involved political contributions associated with the 1996 temple event attended by Al Gore.

**Evidence:** the conviction history and temple association are **Strong** in official case records; the brief is the government's account as a litigant, not a substitute for every judicial finding. **Map description should emphasize:** reimbursed contributions and the documented legal outcome. Do not extend Hsia's convictions into a claim that Gore was convicted, or that the case proved a larger foreign-control narrative. Congressional majority and minority accounts disagreed on important characterizations; use judicial/FEC records for the core description.

**Next records:** read the final appellate disposition and relevant FEC findings together before writing a longer interpretation. The temple has visitor information; check current arrangements.

**Sources read:** [temple contact page](https://www.hsilai.us/about-us/contact-us); [DOJ Supreme Court brief](https://www.justice.gov/osg/brief/hsia-v-united-states-opposition). **Further primary record located:** [FEC Statement of Reasons](https://www.fec.gov/files/legal/murs/4530/00000315.pdf).

## L11 update — Blackburn: a useful legal-history location now exists

The original Wilshire temple address and the Simi retreat parcel remain unresolved. The Homestead Museum's photograph essay narrows the urban headquarters to Wilshire just west of Western; it does not supply a house number.

A separate legal-history study identifies the **Bank of Italy Building, 505 W. 7th Street**. It places Blackburn's December 1929 deposition in the attorneys' office, Suite 530, and places the Dabney oil business in the building. Raymond Chandler worked for that business. This supports an additional **deposition/business-history stop**, not a replacement temple pin or a claim that Chandler joined the group.

Historical support for this particular episode is **Moderate**, from one specialist account with citations. The building address is **Strong** in that account; access to the historic suite is not established. Primary deposition records would strengthen the episode. The same study records the reversal of Blackburn's conviction and subsequent dismissal of charges. Preserve those outcomes whenever the prosecution is discussed.

**Next:** locate the deposition/civil case file, compare the documented suite with a period directory, and keep the original temple/retreat lead open. Golden Gopher (B10) is an optional downtown circuit pairing.

**Sources read:** [Homestead Museum photograph essay](https://homesteadmuseum.blog/2020/10/07/take-it-on-faith-a-press-photo-of-members-of-the-divine-order-of-the-royal-arms-of-the-great-eleven-los-angeles-7-october-1929/); [CSCHS Newsletter, Spring/Summer 2018, printed pages 21–22](https://www.cschs.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/2018-Newsletter-Spring.pdf). Historical support is **Strong**, with institutional documentation and published court accounts. Geographic support remains **Limited** beyond the city/area: none of the sources inspected here established the street address of the burgled office. **Next:** obtain a 1971 physician/business directory and corroborate the office against the original local burglary report or trial exhibit. The nine-page August 1971 Hoover investigation PDF linked from the Nixon page was opened but did not resolve that location. This does not imply the address is absent from all FBI or trial files.

## L14 update — Burnell: the current address is resolved

The foundation's own site lists **1020 Burnell Oaks Lane, Arcadia**. Its history says classes met at the Burnell home on Foothill Boulevard and that the foundation remains on part of that land. UCSB's collection description independently identifies ARC Mss 10 as the Burnells' lecture typescripts, pamphlets, and other materials.

Existence and present address support are **Strong**. The historic house's exact footprint and continuity of particular buildings are **Moderate**, relying on the foundation's narrative until directories and parcel history are checked. This is a New Thought/institutional-history candidate, not an allegation of misconduct.

**Next:** identify the dated Foothill street number in the lecture materials and reconcile it with the present lane and subdivided parcel. Do not use the foundation's modern mailing address as proof of the exact nineteenth-/early-twentieth-century classroom. No public visiting schedule was established.

**Sources read:** [foundation history and address](https://burnellfoundation.org/about/); [UCSB manuscript-collection overview](https://www.library.ucsb.edu/special-collections/research/arcmss).
